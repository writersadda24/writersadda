# Technical writing deliverables and use cases

Technical writing covers far more than manuals. Teams commission content to help people learn a product, complete a procedure, integrate a system, make a decision, meet an internal control, or maintain shared knowledge. The deliverable should follow the reader's task and the source of truth.

## Developer and engineering documentation

| Asset | Helps the reader… | Typical contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| API reference | Understand and call an interface | Operations, parameters, schemas, authentication, responses, errors, examples |
| SDK and integration guide | Connect a product to a development environment | Installation, setup, configuration, code samples, callbacks, troubleshooting |
| Developer quickstart or tutorial | Reach a first useful result | Prerequisites, ordered steps, expected output, next steps |
| Architecture and system overview | Understand components and relationships | Context, architecture diagrams, dependencies, data flow, boundaries |
| Deployment and operations runbook | Deploy, monitor, recover, or maintain a service | Preconditions, commands, checks, rollback, escalation, ownership |
| README, changelog, and release notes | Orient to a repository or understand product changes | Purpose, setup, contribution, version changes, compatibility notes |

## Product, customer, and user documentation

- User guides, task-based help, and knowledge base articles
- Installation, configuration, migration, upgrade, and administration guides
- In-product guidance, contextual help, FAQs, and troubleshooting trees
- Product onboarding and learning paths
- Accessibility and localization-ready content structures
- Customer and partner implementation guides

## Operational, safety, and controlled content

- Standard operating procedures (SOPs), work instructions, and checklists
- Maintenance, inspection, installation, and service procedures
- Incident response, disaster recovery, business continuity, and escalation runbooks
- Quality processes, audit-supporting records, and policy frameworks
- Laboratory methods, clinical or device instructions, and training materials
- Internal knowledge bases, employee onboarding, and process documentation

The content owner must confirm authoritative sources, required approvals, retention rules, and applicable regulations. A writer can structure and clarify content; the client's qualified reviewers remain responsible for domain decisions and formal approval.

## Training, enablement, and learning

- Instructor-led or self-paced course materials
- Tutorials, exercises, labs, and assessment guides
- Product enablement and implementation playbooks
- Technical onboarding for employees, partners, or service teams
- Training scripts, facilitator notes, and job aids

These materials benefit from instructional design: define the learner, prerequisite knowledge, observable objectives, practice opportunities, and evaluation method.

## Technical marketing and decision support

- White papers, solution briefs, technical explainers, architecture overviews, data sheets, and product brochures
- RFP/RFI responses and technical questionnaire support
- Product comparison and implementation guidance
- Customer case studies, when the customer approves the claims and attribution
- Technical sections of proposals, business cases, and product or market-requirements documents (with product ownership retained by the responsible team)

Marketing and proposal content must distinguish verified product facts from positioning, assumptions, roadmap statements, and customer-approved outcomes. A writer can help clarify or structure product requirements; ownership of product strategy and market decisions stays with the responsible business team.

## Content operations and platform work

- Content inventories, audits, and information architecture
- Terminology, style, templates, and governance rules
- Migration from PDFs, wikis, or legacy repositories
- Modular content and reuse models
- Publishing pipelines, review workflows, and maintenance plans
- Localization preparation and translation handoff

## Choosing the right output

Start with the job the reader needs to do, then decide what format best supports it. A short checklist may serve a field task better than a handbook; an API schema can complement prose; a searchable help center may outperform a long PDF for frequent software changes. The product, reader context, risks, and maintenance model determine the right mix.
