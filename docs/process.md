# How we work

Good documentation comes from pairing clear writing with accurate product knowledge. The process keeps the scope visible and gives your team structured chances to review.

## 1. Understand the need

We discuss the product, reader, current documentation, and the problem you want to solve. We identify access needs, stakeholders, and a useful first deliverable.

## 2. Agree on scope

We define what is included, what is out of scope, the source format, review milestones, review rounds, completion criteria, and who is responsible for each input. We also agree on access and how success will be judged.

## 3. Research and outline

We inspect the product and existing material, interview subject-matter experts where needed, then share an outline or content plan before drafting.

## 4. Draft and review

We write for the reader's tasks and mark questions or assumptions for review. Your technical reviewers validate product behavior; we incorporate feedback and resolve open points.

## 5. Deliver and hand over

We deliver in the agreed format with notes on unresolved issues and suggested maintenance. For docs-as-code work, this can include Markdown source and a pull request workflow, depending on your tools. The handoff includes the final source files and any agreed implementation notes.

## Quality checks

Checks depend on the deliverable and available access. They may include link and structure review, terminology checks, and testing procedures or examples in an appropriate environment. Any limitations are stated clearly.
